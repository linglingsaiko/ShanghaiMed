If you're reading this, you've probably already decided that China is worth considering for your treatment — the prices make sense, and you've seen that top hospitals here are genuinely excellent. The next question is harder: who should help you make it happen?

Search online and you'll find many agencies offering China medical tourism services. Their websites all look professional. But the quality behind them varies enormously, and the differences don't show on the homepage — they show in how they answer a few practical questions. Here are the six we'd ask, based on over a decade of working in Shanghai's high-end healthcare sector.

## 1. "Will someone carefully review my medical records before recommending a doctor?"

This is the most important question on the list.

Ask how the agency handles your records. Do they do a thorough review and summary of your complete medical file, and only then recommend a hospital and doctor based on your records together with your chief complaint? Or do they glance at your file and match you to a department based on your complaint alone?

A recommendation made without a detailed record review can send you to the wrong specialist entirely. We once worked with a client who came to us after her previous agency couldn't arrange accommodation that met her needs. We searched across Shanghai until we found housing that worked for her. Then, when we received her medical records and carefully read through all 30-plus pages alongside her chief complaint, we found something important: going by her complaint alone, the surgical specialty her previous agency had recommended seemed like a perfect match. Only after reading the complete file did we see that what she actually needed was internal medicine — autoimmune care, specifically.

Your chief complaint tells part of the story. Your complete records tell the rest. This kind of preparation before you fly saves enormous time, money, and exhausting back-and-forth in a foreign country — especially when you're the patient.

## 2. "Is on-site support billed by the hour, or covered for the full day?"

A hospital visit in China rarely runs to a fixed schedule. Registration, imaging, the consultation, follow-up tests, the pharmacy — the day unfolds at its own pace, and waiting is simply part of the process. Nobody can tell you at 9 AM exactly when you'll be done.

If your on-site escort is billed by the hour, every delay quietly becomes a source of anxiety. You'll find yourself watching the clock when you should be focused on your health. Ask this question early, and get the answer in writing: is your coordinator with you for the full day, however long it takes, or is there a meter running?

## 3. "What will my family members cost, all-in?"

Medical travel is rarely solo travel. Spouses, children, sometimes parents come along — and they all need to sleep, eat, and get around for the length of your stay.

Some platforms price companions as add-ons: per-person fees, booking service charges, per-transfer costs. Individually these look small. Run the total for a whole family over several weeks and the picture changes completely. Before you commit, ask for one number that covers everyone who's traveling with you.

## 4. "Beyond the hospital — who plans how we actually live?"

Here's what most people underestimate: the medical part of a medical trip is maybe 30% of the journey. The rest is daily life. For a stay of several weeks, you're not a tourist — you're a temporary resident.

We once arranged a one-month stay in Shanghai for a family of three from the Middle East. What they needed most wasn't a hotel room — it was an apartment with a proper kitchen so they could cook their own meals, in a neighborhood with a large supermarket within a five-minute walk and halal ingredients easy to source nearby.

But a good location goes beyond the kitchen. We chose an apartment near a metro station, so the family could get around the city freely without depending on taxis. A park within walking distance gave the patient somewhere green and quiet for daily walks during recovery. And a major shopping mall close by meant the family could handle everything from groceries to an occasional normal evening out — because a month abroad shouldn't feel like a month spent in a waiting room.

That kind of arrangement doesn't come from a hotel booking link. It comes from someone who knows the city's neighborhoods and checks the details before you land.

Ask the agency to walk you through how they handle housing, food, and local transport for your specific situation. The specificity of their answer tells you everything.

## 5. "Can I see the full cost breakdown — and what's not included?"

Every agency can show you an attractive package price. The skill is reading what's excluded.

A complete quote should have three parts: what you'll pay the hospital directly, what you'll pay the agency as a service fee, and a realistic estimate of your living costs during the stay. If a platform only shows you the middle number, you can't budget properly — and surprises are almost guaranteed. Ask for all three, in writing, before you pay anything.

## 6. "Who will physically be with me on treatment day — and are they based locally?"

Some agencies selling China medical tourism are actually overseas sales offices. The person who closes your contract may never meet the person who stands next to you at the hospital.

This matters most when plans change: a rescheduled appointment, an unexpected test, a form that needs a quick decision. In those moments, you want someone who is based in the city, knows the hospital's departments by name, and has walked those corridors many times — not a customer service line in another time zone. Ask who your coordinator will be, where they're based, and whether they'll be physically present for your key appointments.

## The Checklist

Before you sign with any agency, get clear answers to these six questions:

1. Will my complete medical records be carefully reviewed before any doctor is recommended?
2. Is my on-site support a full-day arrangement or an hourly meter?
3. What's the all-in cost for everyone traveling with me?
4. Who plans housing, food, and transport — and how specific can they get?
5. Can I see the full cost breakdown, including what's excluded?
6. Who shows up for me on treatment day, and where are they based?

The right partner will answer all six comfortably and in writing. Take your time, ask the questions, and choose the team that treats you like a patient they're responsible for — not a booking they're processing.

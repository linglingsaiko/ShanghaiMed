Fifteen years of contact lenses. Fifteen years of "I should really get LASIK." And every time, the same thing stopped him: the price.

An accountant from Chicago in his early thirties, he'd worn contacts since he was seventeen. Every morning: dry eyes, blurry vision, fumbling for solution. Every year: the same Google search, the same sticker shock. Chicago quoted $4,800 for both eyes. New York: $5,700. Insurance covered zero.

Then he started planning a trip to China — not for surgery, just for the Bund, the food, the whole experience. He messaged our team: "I heard eye surgery in Shanghai is way cheaper. Is that true?"

It was. SMILE — the latest laser technique — using the same Zeiss equipment found in top US clinics, at roughly a third of the price. And he could do it during his Shanghai trip. No separate medical journey needed.

"Do I need a medical visa?" he asked.

No. SMILE is an outpatient procedure — no hospitalisation required. He'd sorted his 10-year multi-entry tourist visa before departure, the standard arrangement for Americans visiting China. Only surgeries involving hospital admission require extra paperwork. His trip — land, evaluate, operate, recover, holiday — stayed fully compliant.

He booked the same week.

## Day 2: Evaluation

He landed at Pudong on a Tuesday evening. Our team met him at arrivals, drove him to a hotel near one of Shanghai's leading eye hospitals.

Next morning, straight to the hospital. We walked in with him, translated the intake forms, sat beside him during the evaluation.

The refractive surgeon — a chief physician with years of specialised experience — ran every scan: corneal topography, thickness, pupil diameter. Fifteen minutes of tests. Then the verdict: thick, regular corneas. Perfect candidate for SMILE. Surgery scheduled for the next morning.

"In Chicago, I'd wait three weeks just for the consultation," he said.

## Day 3: Surgery

Thursday morning. Back at the hospital. The surgery took about 15 minutes. Local anaesthetic drops only. No scalpel, no flap — the Zeiss VisuMax 800 laser carved a tiny lens inside the cornea, and the surgeon removed it through a 3mm incision. Less than ten seconds of laser time per eye.

He walked out squinting. "Like sand in my eyes. But I can already see shapes."

Back at the hotel by noon. Eye drops, instructions, and a firm order: sleep.

## Days 4–6: Recovery and Shanghai

Friday morning — about 24 hours after surgery — functional vision was back. He looked out the hotel window.

"I can see the buildings. Without my contacts. For the first time since I was seventeen."

By afternoon he was exploring — Yu Garden's pavilions, koi ponds, stone pathways. Day 5: the Bund at sunset, the Lujiazui skyline across the Huangpu River. He kept blinking in disbelief: "Everything is so sharp."

Our team checked in daily. "Any pain? How's the vision?" Better every morning.

## Day 7: Follow-up

Back at the hospital. Post-op scans.

"Both eyes 20/20. Corneal healing excellent."

"20/20? For real?"

"For real. Use the drops for two weeks. No swimming for a month. Otherwise — you're done."

He turned to us: "I spent fifteen years telling myself I'd get this done. It took fifteen minutes."

## Why Shanghai for eye surgery?

This isn't about "cheap." It's about volume, technology, and experience.

**Equipment:** The hospital uses the Zeiss VisuMax 800 — the latest generation, the same machine found in top US clinics. Many practices still use the previous generation 500 model.

**Volume:** The senior team has performed over 260,000 SMILE surgeries — more than any single centre worldwide, a record held for consecutive years.

**Outcomes:** Results at this centre are comparable to the best published international results.

**Recovery:** SMILE is flapless. Most patients see functionally within 24 hours. He was sightseeing about 24 hours post-surgery. Traditional LASIK creates a corneal flap and needs a week of careful recovery.

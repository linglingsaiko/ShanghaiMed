## Top Medical Procedures for US Travelers

### 1. Orthopedic Surgery
Knee/hip replacement, spinal fusion, and sports injury treatment at **Sixth People's Hospital** — site of the world's first successful limb replantation (1963) — and **Huashan Hospital**. Internationally used implant brands such as Stryker and Zimmer are available; the specific model and suitability depend on the hospital, surgeon, and your clinical assessment. Costs are typically lower than in the US.

### 2. Cosmetic & Plastic Surgery
Rhinoplasty, breast augmentation, and facial reconstruction at **Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital** — ranked #1 in China for plastic surgery. Savings of 65-75%.

### 3. Dental Treatment
Implants (Straumann brand), crowns, and orthodontics at **Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital** — China's #1 dental hospital with 100+ dental chairs and a dedicated International Medical Department. A dental implant costs $800-$1,200 vs $4,000-$6,000 in the US.

### 4. Traditional Chinese Medicine
Authentic acupuncture, herbal therapy, and Tuina massage at **Longhua Hospital** — a national TCM flagship with 1,000+ registered formulas. One month of acupuncture costs $200-$400 vs $1,000-$2,000 in the US.

### 5. Cancer Treatment
Chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy at **Huashan Hospital** and **Ruijin Hospital**. Ruijin has an established CAR-T cell therapy program for certain blood cancers.

### 6. Cardiac Surgery
Coronary bypass, valve replacement, and interventional cardiology at **Zhongshan Hospital** (Fudan University), which features an independent international patient building and 19 insurance partners. $11,000-$21,000 vs $80,000-$150,000 in the US.

### 7. Fertility Treatment
IVF and ICSI at **Renji Hospital** (SJTU) and **International Peace Maternity & Child Health Hospital**, at substantially lower cost than in the US (specific outcomes depend on individual diagnosis and treatment plan).

### 8. Executive Health Screening
Comprehensive full-day checkup at **Huadong Hospital** (Fudan University) — a Bupa Silver certified center. $300-$600 vs $2,000-$5,000 in the US. Includes bloodwork, imaging, specialist consultations, and personalized health report.

50-80% on procedures. A knee replacement costs $5,000-$10,000 in Shanghai vs $30,000-$50,000 in the US. A cardiac bypass costs $11,000-$21,000 vs $80,000-$150,000. Even with flights and accommodation, total trip cost is often less than the US procedure alone.

### Q3: Do doctors speak English?

### Q4: Can I use my US health insurance?
Possibly. Some hospitals have direct settlement arrangements with international insurers, but availability depends on the hospital, insurer, policy, and pre-authorization. ShanghaiMed assists with insurance verification before your trip and does not guarantee coverage or direct settlement.

### Q5: Do I always need a medical visa?
No. Health checks, dental care, TCM, ordinary outpatient visits, follow-up appointments, and second opinions may use a tourist visa or an applicable visa-free policy. Planned surgery, hospital admission, or other inpatient treatment generally requires an **S2 visa** supported by the treating hospital's medical invitation or admission documents, or an eligible change of status after lawful entry. The 240-hour transit visa exemption covers **57 eligible countries** for qualifying short outpatient trips, not planned hospital admission. Confirm current rules with the National Immigration Administration or **+86-12367** before travel.

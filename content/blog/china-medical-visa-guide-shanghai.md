## Quick Answer: Which Visa Do I Need?

If you only have 30 seconds, this decision tree covers most situations:

| Your situation | Visa you need | Can you enter visa-free? |
|---|---|---|
| Health checkup / dental / TCM / second opinion only, no admission | **Tourist (L) visa or visa-free entry** (30 days) | ✅ Yes, if your nationality qualifies (see §3) |
| Planned surgery, hospital admission, or multi-day inpatient treatment | **S2 visa (Short-term Private Affairs, medical purpose)** | ❌ No — you must apply in advance with a hospital invitation letter |
| Malaysian or Kazakhstani citizen, treatment within 30 days | **Visa-free** (bilateral agreements explicitly include medical purposes) | ✅ Yes — up to 30 days, 90 days per 180-day period |
| Already in China on any visa and a medical issue is discovered that needs treatment | **No exit required** — apply to convert/extend your visa domestically at the Exit-Entry Bureau | See §4 |
| Treatment expected to exceed 180 days | **S1 visa → converted to a residence permit within 30 days of entry** | ❌ No |

> **Important clarification:** China does not have a visa category literally named "Medical Visa." For planned medical treatment, the correct category is the **S2 (Short-term Private Affairs) visa with a medical treatment purpose**. Hospitals and visa agencies colloquially call this the "medical visa" — and functionally, that is exactly what it is.
>
> — *Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreign Nationals (2013 Order No. 637, as amended by State Council Order No. 814, effective October 1, 2025), Art. 6 (S2 category)*; [Chinese Embassy in Kazakhstan, Visa Application Notice (Apr 2026)](https://kz.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/zhengjian/qianzheng/202604/t20260424_11899222.htm)

## 1. Understanding China's Visa System for Medical Travelers

Before diving into specifics, it helps to understand how China classifies foreign visitors. China's visa system uses letter categories, each tied to a specific purpose of visit. There are currently 13 ordinary visa types: C (crew), D (permanent residence), F (exchange/visit), G (transit), J (journalists), K (young tech talent), L (tourism), M (business/trade), Q (family reunion — visiting Chinese citizens/permanent residents), R (high-level talent), S (private affairs/family — visiting foreigners residing in China), X (study), and Z (work).

Medical treatment falls under **S1/S2 — Private Affairs**. The "S" stands for "sīrén shìwù" (私人事务), meaning "private matters." Within the S2 category, Chinese embassies and visa centers maintain a well-defined subcategory specifically for medical treatment, with its own documentary requirements (hospital letters, bank deposits, escort rules). Anyone planning inpatient care or surgery should use this route.

This distinction matters because Chinese immigration authorities take visa-purpose compliance seriously. **Article 81 of the Exit-Entry Administration Law** allows for deportation of foreigners who engage in activities inconsistent with their visa purpose. However — and this is a critical nuance that most online guides get wrong — the law also provides humane and practical mechanisms for handling medical situations that arise after entry. We cover these in Sections 4 and 5.

> **Sources:** [Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreign Nationals (2013 Order No. 637, as amended by State Council Order No. 814)](https://ch.china-embassy.gov.cn/ls_fw_s/flfgmqxy/202608/t20260821_12008307.htm); [Exit-Entry Administration Law of the PRC, Art. 81](http://www.jieyang.gov.cn/jyga/xxgk/zcfg/fgwj/content/post_1013208.html)

## 2. The S2 Visa — The Main Route for Planned Medical Treatment

The S2 visa with a medical purpose is the standard route for any foreign national planning hospital treatment in China, including at Shanghai's top Grade 3A public hospitals and international medical centers.

### What You Need to Prepare

Based on current requirements published by Chinese embassies and visa application service centers worldwide, prepare the following:

**Core documents (all applicants):**
1. Original passport valid for at least 6 months with at least 2 blank visa pages
2. One photocopy of the passport's data page
3. A completed online visa application form with 1 photo (33mm × 48mm, white background, recently taken)
4. Proof of legal residence in a third country (if applying outside your country of nationality — 180+ days of legal stay)
5. Previous Chinese visas, if any (photocopies help)
6. Former Chinese citizens holding foreign passports: additional documents as required by the embassy

**Medical treatment — key documents:**
1. A **hospital invitation letter, admission notice, or proof of proposed admission** issued by the treating hospital in China (some embassies also request a copy of the hospital's medical practice license)
2. **Original bank statement or bank certificate showing a balance of at least USD 10,000** (or equivalent)
3. The patient's medical diagnosis certificate and any referral/transfer documents, where applicable

**Escorts (family members accompanying the patient):**
- Escorts must be **immediate family members only**: spouse, parents, parents-in-law, children, children's spouses, siblings, grandparents, maternal/paternal grandparents, grandchildren, and grandchildren-in-law
- **Friends, nannies, professional caregivers, and assistants do NOT qualify for S2 escort visas**
- Maximum of **2 escorts per patient**
- Required documents: proof of kinship (marriage certificate, birth certificate, notarized relationship certificate) + a hospital escort letter

**Minor patients (under 18):**
- Both parents/legal guardians must sign the application form
- Original birth certificate (with parents' details) + photocopy
- Translation required if the birth certificate is not in Chinese, English, or Russian
- If neither parent accompanies the minor: a notarized power of attorney is required
- Photocopies of both parents' passports and legal status documents

> **Practical note:** The invitation letter may be a fax, photocopy, or printed scan — an original is not required unless specifically requested by the consular officer. This means the hospital can email or fax the letter to you without international courier delays.
>
> — *Sources:* [China Visa Application Service Center, Tashkent (S2 medical document requirements)](https://bio.visaforchina.cn/TAS4_ZH/qianzhengyewu/jichuzhishi/banliliucheng/552382671075020818.html); [Chinese Embassy in Kazakhstan (Apr 2026 update)](https://kz.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/zhengjian/qianzheng/202604/t20260424_11899222.htm); [Chinese Embassy in Cameroon (visa notice, Jun 2026)](https://cm.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lqfw/lszj/fhqz/202305/t20230511_11075268.htm)

### How to Apply

Since **June 30, 2025**, all Chinese visa applications use the unified online system: [https://consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA/](https://consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA/). The older bio.visaforchina.cn form-filling system is being phased out, but physical document submission and biometric collection still take place at visa application centers in many cities.

**Processing times** (standard):
- Regular: **4 working days**
- Express: **3 working days** (additional fee)
- Rush: **1–2 working days** (for genuinely urgent medical situations, with the hospital letter explicitly stating urgency)
- Emergency humanitarian visa: same-day or next-working-day issuance for critical cases, with a hospital letter documenting a life-threatening condition requiring treatment within 7 days

### Current Policy Discounts (Extended Through December 31, 2026)

Two temporary policies remain in force and benefit medical travelers:

1. **Visa fee reduction** — many nationalities pay reduced visa fees under the current extension.
2. **Fingerprint exemption for short-term visas** — applicants for visas with a stay of 180 days or less are temporarily exempt from providing fingerprints in person (through December 31, 2026). Certain categories are permanently exempt: children under 14, adults over 70, diplomatic passport holders, applicants who have submitted fingerprints on the same passport at the same mission within the past 5 years, and applicants with missing/damaged fingers.

> **Sources:** [Chinese Embassy in Laos (visa notice, Jun 2025)](https://la.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/zshfl/lszjfhqz/202506/t20250621_11654570.htm); [Chinese Consulate General in St. Petersburg](https://saintpetersburg.china-consulate.gov.cn/chn/lsyw/lszj/fuqz/sbqzxz/)

### How to Get the Hospital Invitation Letter

The invitation letter is the document that unlocks the entire S2 process, and it requires coordination before you ever set foot in a consulate. You cannot simply call a hospital and ask for one; a physician must first review your case and agree to accept you as a patient.

**The typical process (4–8 weeks total):**
1. Submit complete medical records (imaging, pathology, surgical reports, current medication list) — translated into Chinese clinical format
2. The hospital's International Medical Department routes your file to the relevant specialty
3. A chief physician reviews your case (5–10 working days), confirming treatability and providing a preliminary treatment opinion
4. **For complex or surgical cases**, a video consultation with the treating physician may be scheduled so the doctor can confirm the treatment plan
5. Once the physician agrees to accept you, the International Department issues the formal invitation letter with the hospital's official seal and an authorized physician's signature (2–5 working days)

**The letter must include:** patient's full name, gender, date of birth, passport number, diagnosis (in Chinese/English), proposed treatment/surgery, estimated admission dates and duration, hospital contact details, the authorized physician's signature, and the hospital seal.

If you anticipate multiple entries (e.g., staged surgeries, chemo cycles, or follow-up procedures), **the invitation letter must explicitly state the staged treatment plan and expected return dates**. This enables the consular officer to issue a multiple-entry S2 visa valid for 6–12 months.

> **A note on public hospitals:** Shanghai's top Grade 3A public hospitals generally do not schedule definitive surgery without an in-person consultation. However, their International Medical Departments can issue invitation letters based on written records plus a video consultation. This is standard practice and is legally sufficient as the basis for S2 visa issuance.

## 3. The Truth About Visa-Free Entry — What Works and What Doesn't

China has dramatically expanded visa-free access in recent years, and this has created understandable confusion about whether you can simply fly in for medical care. The honest answer depends on what you intend to do.

### A. The 30-Day Unilateral Visa-Free Policy (50 Countries)

As of October 2026, holders of ordinary passports from **50 countries** may enter China without a visa for stays of up to 30 days for business, tourism, visiting family, exchange visits, or transit. The 50 countries are:

**Europe (35):** France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Portugal, Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Latvia, Luxembourg, Malta, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia, Sweden, Switzerland, Monaco, Norway, Andorra, Liechtenstein, UK, Ireland, Cyprus, Bulgaria, Romania, Croatia, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Russia

**Asia (3):** Brunei (permanent/no expiry), Japan, South Korea

**Gulf States (West Asia) (4):** Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain

**Oceania (2):** New Zealand, Australia

**Americas (6):** Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Peru, Uruguay, Canada

This policy is valid through **December 31, 2026** for 48 countries (including Sweden, added November 10, 2025); **Russia** is separately extended through **December 31, 2027**; **Brunei** has no expiration date.

> ⚠️ **The critical detail for medical travelers:** The listed permitted purposes are business, tourism, family visits, exchange, and transit — **medical treatment is not listed**. This does not, however, mean you cannot see a doctor during a visit.

> **Sources:** [National Immigration Administration (NIA) announcements](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/index.html); [Ministry of Culture and Tourism, notice on extending unilateral visa-free policy for Russia (May 2026)](https://www.mct.gov.cn/)

**What visa-free entry works fine for:**
- ✅ Health screenings, executive checkups, and preventive examinations (typically completed in 1–2 days, no admission required)
- ✅ Outpatient dental work, TCM consultations, and second-opinion visits within your 30-day window
- ✅ Tourist activities plus a medical appointment (the vast majority of "medical tourism" for checkups falls into this category)

**What you should not attempt on visa-free entry:**
- ❌ Planned inpatient admission, surgery, or multi-week treatment protocols requiring a hospital admission letter. These require the S2 visa arranged in advance.

### B. The 240-Hour Transit Visa-Free Policy (57 Countries)

Most recently extended on August 20, 2026 (adding Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam), the 240-hour transit visa-free policy covers 57 countries and 65 entry ports, including Shanghai Pudong, Shanghai Hongqiao, and the Shanghai port. Visitors may stay up to 240 hours (10 days) within the port's administrative area (from Shanghai, movement is allowed across Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, and Shanghai).

The listed purposes are tourism, business, visits, and family — **not medical treatment**. This policy is designed for transit stays, and you should not plan medical treatment around it. If you are transiting through Shanghai and want to squeeze in a quick health checkup or dental visit during your 10-day window, this is practically workable. Do not use transit visa-free entry for planned medical treatment.

> **Source:** [NIA Announcement No. 4 of 2026 (August 20, 2026)](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c180637/content.html)

### C. Bilateral Visa-Free Agreements — The Special Cases

Two bilateral agreements explicitly include "medical treatment" (医疗) as a permitted purpose; a third (Thailand) permits private affairs short visits with the same practical effect for outpatient care:

| Country | Effective date | Max stay per visit | Max stay per 180 days | Medical purpose listed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaysia** | July 17, 2025 | ≤30 days | ≤90 days | ✅ Yes — explicitly |
| **Kazakhstan** | November 10, 2023 | ≤30 days | ≤90 days | ✅ Yes — explicitly |
| **Thailand** | March 1, 2024 | ≤30 days | ≤90 days | ⚠️ Not explicitly listed (short private visits generally accepted; surgery requires S2) |

Additionally, the **UAE** has had a comprehensive mutual visa-free agreement with China since January 16, 2018 (up to 30 days per stay, up to 90 days per 180-day period, subject to immigration stamping on arrival) — the first such agreement between China and a Middle Eastern country. The agreement text does not explicitly enumerate "medical" like the Malaysian and Kazakhstani agreements, but 30-day health checkups and outpatient visits are routinely completed visa-free; inpatient treatment requires a domestic conversion to S2.

Singapore also has a bilateral visa-free agreement with China (effective February 9, 2024, up to 30 days per stay). Like Thailand and the UAE, the agreement does not explicitly enumerate "medical" as a purpose, but health checkups and outpatient visits are routinely completed visa-free.

For **Malaysian and Kazakhstani patients** — two of ShanghaiMed's key markets — this is a meaningful advantage: checkups, dental work, outpatient consultations, and even minor procedures can be completed entirely visa-free within 30 days.

> **Sources:** [Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Table of Mutual Visa Exemption Agreements (updated May 29, 2026)](https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmcgx/cgqzapecswlxk/ylb/202607/t20260716_11984014.html); [Chinese Embassy in Malaysia, visa-free notice](https://my.china-embassy.gov.cn/fwzc/lsyw/lszj/fhqz2024/sbqzxz/202506/t20250606_11642531.htm); [Chinese Embassy in Kazakhstan (Apr 2026 update)](https://kz.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/zhengjian/qianzheng/202604/t20260424_11899222.htm)

### D. The Hainan Regional Visa-Free Policy (61 Countries, 30 Days)

Hainan province's visa-free policy explicitly includes "medical treatment" as a permitted purpose, reflecting Hainan's positioning as a medical tourism pilot zone (notably the Boao Lecheng International Medical Tourism Pilot Zone). However, this visa-free stay is limited to Hainan province only — it cannot be used to travel to Shanghai for treatment. We list it here for completeness.

> **Source:** [NIA Regional Visa-Free Entry Policy (August 20, 2026)](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147463/c180637/content.html)

## 4. What If You Discover Something Mid-Trip? The In-Country Conversion Path

This is the scenario international patients misunderstand most, and the one where many online guides give dangerous advice — suggesting you must fly out of China to apply for a new visa if you fall ill after entering.

**You do not need to leave China.**

**Legal basis:** Article 10 of the *Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreign Nationals* (2013 Order No. 637, as amended by Order No. 814) explicitly states: "After foreign nationals enter China on a visa, where they may change the purpose of stay in accordance with national provisions or be granted entry facilities, they may apply to the Exit-Entry Administration of the local public security organ at or above the county level at their place of stay for visa reissuance."

Here is what that means in practice:

| Scenario | What to do | Need to leave China? |
|---|---|---|
| Entered visa-free or on a tourist visa; a doctor discovers a condition requiring admission/surgery | Hospital issues a formal diagnosis and proposed admission certificate; you (or your medical concierge) take this to the Exit-Entry Bureau to convert to an S2-type stay permit (30–90 days, renewable) | **No** |
| Entered on S2; treatment takes longer than expected | Apply for an S2 extension with a new hospital letter (30–90 days per extension), processing ~7 working days | **No** |
| Entered visa-free; need to extend beyond the 30-day window for medical reasons | Apply for a stay permit at the Exit-Entry Bureau on humanitarian/medical grounds | **No** |
| Treatment expected to exceed 6 months (chemotherapy, rehabilitation, etc.) | Apply for a Private Affairs Residence Permit (up to 1 year per issuance), requiring a hospital certificate (Grade 2A or above in China's hospital classification system) confirming 6+ months of treatment | **No** |
| Emergency admission (any visa or visa-free) | Treatment always comes first. The hospital will admit and stabilize you regardless of visa status. Visa formalities are handled concurrently by the hospital's international department or your medical concierge. | **No** |

**Who can apply on your behalf:** Article 19 of the Regulations allows the inviting entity, relatives, or **"designated professional service agencies"** to submit applications on behalf of a foreigner who is ill or otherwise unable to apply in person. This is the legal basis on which a medical concierge service can handle your visa extension while you focus on recovery.

**Shanghai Exit-Entry Administration:** 1500 Minsheng Road, Pudong New Area. Processing: 7 working days for visa extensions/reissuance; 15 working days for residence permits. The national 24-hour multilingual hotline (Mandarin/Cantonese/English/Russian/Japanese/Korean) is **+86-12367**.

> **Sources:** [Regulations on the Administration of the Entry and Exit of Foreign Nationals, Arts. 10, 16, 19](http://www.jieyang.gov.cn/jyga/xxgk/zcfg/fgwj/content/post_1013208.html); [Changchun Public Security Bureau, visa document guide (Jul 2026)](http://gaj.changchun.gov.cn/crj/gfqwzcwj/202607/t20260715_3500845.html); [NIA 12367 service platform](https://www.nia.gov.cn/)

## 5. Port Visas and Emergency Humanitarian Situations

For genuinely time-critical medical emergencies where there is no time to apply at an embassy abroad, China's port visa system provides a safety net.

**Port visas** are available at **99 ports of entry across 73 cities**, including Shanghai Pudong and Hongqiao airports. For humanitarian reasons (including emergency medical treatment), you or your inviting party (e.g., the receiving hospital) can apply for an S2 port visa on arrival. Processing time: **1 working day**. The port visa is typically issued as a single-entry visa valid for up to 30 days. The application can be pre-submitted by the hospital before your arrival, or handled on landing.

**Emergency humanitarian visa processing** at Chinese embassies abroad applies to urgent situations: funerals, visiting critically ill relatives, or emergency medical treatment. With a hospital letter documenting the life-threatening urgency, processing can be expedited to **1–3 working days**, and in extreme cases same-day issuance. Contact the consular section of the nearest Chinese embassy by email before submitting, requesting urgent processing.

> **Sources:** [Port Visa Issuance Service Guide, Fujian Provincial Public Security Department](https://gat.fj.gov.cn/ztzl/cryjfwwj/bsxz_28282/202110/t20211008_5700603.htm); [Port Visa Q&A, Changchun Public Security Bureau (Jul 2026)](http://gaj.changchun.gov.cn/crj/gfqwzcwj/202607/t20260715_3500839.html)

## 6. Special Situations

### Escorts: Family vs. Nannies/Aides

The S2 escort visa subcategory is strictly limited to immediate family members (spouse, parents, parents-in-law, children, children's spouses, siblings, grandparents, maternal/paternal grandparents, grandchildren, grandchildren-in-law), with a maximum of **2 escorts per patient**. Friends, professional nannies, domestic workers, personal assistants, and private translators cannot obtain S2 escort visas.

**Practical solutions:**
- For short stays, a nanny or assistant can accompany the patient on a tourist (L) visa
- Once in China, professional medical attendants, translators, and domestic help can be arranged locally through a medical concierge service — avoiding visa issues entirely
- Never misrepresent a relationship to bring a non-family member on an S2 escort visa; material misrepresentation in visa applications can result in long-term or permanent bans

### Bringing Medication Into China

Patients traveling with prescription medications should observe Chinese customs rules:

- **Over-the-counter medications** (cold medicine, antacids, vitamins): keep them in original packaging, bring a reasonable personal-use quantity; no prescription needed
- **Common prescription medications** (blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol medications, antibiotics, SSRIs, insulin, contraceptives): carry the **original prescription** (with drug name, dosage, and patient name matching); a 7-day supply is recommended for short trips, with longer quantities acceptable with supporting documentation; declare via the red channel
- **Controlled substances — exercise extreme caution:** ADHD stimulant medications (Adderall, Vyvanse, Ritalin, Concerta — classified as Category 1 controlled substances in China), strong opioids (OxyContin), and codeine-containing cough syrups are restricted; carry only the maximum quantity for one prescription with a medical diagnosis certificate and ID documents, and **declare via the red channel**. Entry success with ADHD stimulants is not guaranteed; patients should consult their physician about alternatives before travel
- **Cannabis/CBD/THC in any form: strictly prohibited**
- Traditional Chinese medicines containing endangered species ingredients (musk, tiger bone, rhino horn) are also prohibited

> **Sources:** [Beijing Customs, personal medication FAQ (Aug 2026)](http://gdfs.customs.gov.cn/beijing_customs/2026-08/28/article_2026082903061837173.html); [Drug Administration Law Implementation Regulations (effective May 15, 2026)](https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/)

### Treatment Exceeding 180 Days

For treatments expected to exceed 6 months (e.g., extended cancer treatment, long-term rehabilitation), apply for an **S1 visa** before arrival, then convert to a Private Affairs Residence Permit within 30 days of entry. The residence permit requires a medical health check at the designated International Travel Healthcare Center (Shanghai: 15 Jinbang Road, Changning District) for permits of one year or longer. The maximum duration per issuance is 1 year, renewable.

### Multiple-Entry S2 Visas for Staged Treatment

For patients needing staged surgeries (e.g., two-stage orthopedic procedures), multi-cycle chemotherapy, or planned follow-up procedures, the initial hospital invitation letter should explicitly state the staged treatment timeline and expected return dates. Consular officers can then issue a **multiple-entry S2 visa valid for 6 or 12 months**, allowing 30–90 days of stay per entry, without requiring a new visa application for each visit.

### What If My Visa Application Is Refused?

Common reasons for S2 visa refusals include:
- Invitation letter missing required elements (patient details, diagnosis, treatment plan, hospital seal)
- Insufficient financial proof (bank statement below USD 10,000)
- Material misrepresentation or document forgery (the most serious — can result in long-term or permanent visa ineligibility)
- Prior immigration violations in China
- Wrong visa category selection

Applications can be resubmitted after correcting the issue. For urgent medical situations, contact the consular section directly by email to request humanitarian expedited processing. Always ensure documents are genuine and complete; consulates generally approve complete, honest applications.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I just book a health checkup online and fly to Shanghai visa-free?**
Yes, if you hold a passport from one of the 50 unilateral visa-free countries, or from Malaysia/Kazakhstan/Thailand/UAE/Singapore (bilateral visa-free). Health checkups are preventive health services, not inpatient treatment. Book the appointment, bring your passport, and complete the visit within the 30-day window.

**Q: I discovered I need surgery during a checkup. Do I have to fly home and reapply?**
No. As described in Section 4, you can convert/extend your visa domestically at the Shanghai Exit-Entry Bureau with the hospital's diagnosis and admission documentation. Treatment can begin without leaving China. In emergencies, hospitals treat patients first — visa formalities are handled concurrently.

**Q: Can I bring my nanny/caregiver/personal assistant?**
The S2 escort visa is limited to immediate family members (spouse, parents, children, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren; max 2). For professional care or translation support, arrange local services through your medical coordinator after arrival. Nannies and assistants can accompany you short-term on a tourist visa.

**Q: Do I really need USD 10,000 in the bank?**
This is a documentary requirement set by Chinese consulates for the S2 medical visa. It is not a payment — it is a bank certificate demonstrating you can fund your treatment and living costs during your stay. The exact threshold may vary slightly by embassy; check your local Chinese embassy's website before applying.

**Q: Can I apply for a Chinese visa entirely online?**
The application itself is online — since June 30, 2025, all Chinese visa applications use [consular.mfa.gov.cn](https://consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA/). After online submission and approval of the form, you print the confirmation page and submit physical documents (and biometrics, if required) at the designated visa application center. Note: a fully electronic visa system has been announced as in development; check the National Immigration Administration's latest announcements for updates.

**Q: How do I get the hospital invitation letter?**
You must contact the hospital's International Medical Department (or work through a medical concierge service) and submit complete medical records for physician review. You cannot simply call and request one; a doctor must review your case and agree to accept you as a patient. Complex cases may require a video consultation. The full process typically takes 4–8 weeks from submission to receiving the sealed invitation letter.

**Q: Do I need to speak Chinese to get medical care in Shanghai?**
At Shanghai's top Grade 3A public hospitals with international departments, and at dedicated international hospitals, English-speaking coordinators and physicians are available. For other languages (Arabic, Russian, Japanese, etc.), it is strongly advisable to arrange coordination support to ensure clear communication throughout your care. This is one of the core services that medical concierge providers offer.
